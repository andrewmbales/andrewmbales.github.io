Morse Reader Support

Questions, problems or ideas: email MorseReaderApp@gmail.com. If something didn't decode well, include a recording (Settings ▸ Developer ▸ Save last 60 seconds of audio) and what was sent.

Receiving

Quickest: tap Listen and hold your phone near the radio's speaker. The app finds the pitch and speed by itself.

Better: a cable from the radio's speaker jack to your phone's mic input. The phone needs a TRRS (4-conductor) connection: a USB-C or Lightning headset adapter with a TRRS cable, or a TRRS splitter. Turn the radio's volume well down; speaker output is much louder than a microphone, and clipping hurts decoding more than low level does.

If two stations are on at once, tap the pitch you want on the spectrum, or tap Auto to go back.

Sending

Messages play from the headset jack when a cable to the radio's mic input is connected, otherwise from the speaker. Key the radio with its PTT button, or with VOX (turn on VOX lead-in tone in Settings so VOX keys up before the first dot). Start with the output level low: a mic input is sensitive, and too much audio distorts.

With a callsign set in Settings, every send ends with DE and your callsign, and a long send identifies every 10 minutes.

The rules (US)

Listening and decoding are fine anywhere. On GMRS, Morse is for station identification; sending Morse messages over the air needs an amateur radio license. Practising off the air needs nothing.
